### Defects found and fixed by these tests

1. **linfa-logistic positive class** (found by the closed-form baseline). The crate
   documents "probability of the larger class" but `label_classes` selects the class
   with the larger *count* (ties: first encountered). The adapter now reads
   `labels().pos.class` and negates coefficients when class 0 was modelled.
2. **Dummy column order** (found by the differential suite). recipes appends indicator
   columns after the remaining columns; the first implementation inserted them in
   place. Fixed; feature order is now compared exactly.
3. **Rank-deficient folds** (found by the differential suite on Ames). A dummy level
   absent from a fold's analysis rows yields an all-zero column; linfa's least squares
   errors ("Matrix is non-invertible") where R pivots and reports `NA`. The framework now
   drops identically-zero columns, and constant columns when an intercept is fitted,
   before calling any backend, reports them in `FitDiagnostics::aliased`, and assigns
   coefficient 0, which reproduces R's predictions (ADR-0007).

Result: structure, directions, sizes and counts **exact** on every full-data tree and on
all 25 fold trees; split points, deviances, means, class shares, cp and `improve`
within 1.3e-14 relative; assessment predictions and probabilities identical to
round-off; every fold metric, including the tie-sensitive ROC/PR family, within the
regular bounds (differential suite now 4126 comparisons, 0 failures).

Findings on the way, each of which produced a measurable disagreement before it was
fixed and none of which is visible from the final tree alone:

1. **Predictor scale.** With `step_normalize` before the tree, assessment rows whose
   raw value equals a split point (an integer exactly halfway between two training
   values) were routed by the last bit of the baked value, which differs between R and
   Rust; a near-tie between two variables flipped the same way. Tree tasks therefore
   keep raw predictors (trees are invariant to monotone transforms); with raw values
   the baked matrices are bitwise identical in both systems.
2. **Sort order.** rpart's `mysort` is an unstable quicksort; the order of tied values
   fixes the floating-point accumulation order of the split sums and hence which of two
   exactly tied splits wins. The sort is ported literally.
3. **Altered priors.** With default `parms`, `giniinit`'s `aprior` factors are 1 only up
   to an ulp (the class proportions need not sum to exactly 1 in binary), and that ulp
   decided an x2/x3 tie at one node of one fold. The factors are computed as in C.
4. **Class probabilities.** rpart's R code forms `yprob` as `counts × (prior/freq)`
   normalised by `rowSums`, which yields different doubles for the same share (1/6 as
   0.16666666666666666 and 0.16666666666666669 in two nodes); yardstick's ROC/PR curves
   see those as distinct thresholds. The port uses the same arithmetic. Relatedly, the
   fixtures are now written with 17 significant digits: jsonlite's default 15 merged the
   two doubles and changed `roc_auc` by 1e-3 on two folds.

Independent baselines in `crates/tidyrustmodels-models/tests/tree.rs` and the
`native::rpart` unit tests: closed-form splits and leaf means, Gini class shares, the
rpart rule that a split must reduce the risk even at `cp = 0`, first-level tie-breaking,
`minsplit` / `maxdepth` stops, parameter validation, exact state round trip.

Not covered: categorical splits, surrogates, non-default priors/loss, case weights
(pending, priority 6), the cp table and `xval`.

| task | outcome | n test | largest ratio of mean difference to band | predictions within 4 SE | max row |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `synth_reg_rf` | numeric | 10 | 0.31 (rsq) | 100 % | 3.69 SE |
| `ames_reg_rf` (300 trees) | numeric | 733 | 0.19 (mae) | 99.3 % | 4.97 SE |

| `penguins_species_rf` | 3 classes | 86 | 0.48 (log loss) | 98.4 % | 6.31 SE |

`mtry`, `min.node.size` and the tree type agree with ranger's resolved values on all
five tasks. Every metric mean is inside the band; the largest ratio is 0.48 of the
band (log loss on penguins). The band is a detection threshold: with 10 seeds a
systematic bias below about 1.8 standard deviations of a metric's seed-to-seed
spread would pass, e.g. 7e-4 of accuracy on penguins or 5e-2 of rmse on `ames_reg_rf`
(0.2 % of the value).

Two observations were followed up rather than accepted:

1. **Bimodal log loss.** On penguins, three of ten Rust forests scored 0.265 where the
   other seven and all ten R forests scored about 0.61: one test row has a true class
   that gets probability exactly 0 unless some tree puts a same-class training row in
   its leaf, and then `mn_log_loss`'s eps clamp contributes 0.40 to the mean. Three
   of ten against none of ten has a one-sided Fisher p of 0.11, so it was re-run with
   60 ranger seeds (101..160) and 60 Rust seeds: **7 of 60 forests on each side**
   show the low mode (means 0.5732 R, 0.5735 Rust), i.e. the two growers agree on
   how often that leaf is shared. The 60-seed fixture is not kept (it triples the R and
   Rust time); `reference/tidymodels-r/rf_more_seeds.R <task> <from> <to>` regenerates
   it for any forest task.
2. **Rows beyond 4 SE.** With 10 seeds, 4 of 258 penguins values, 5 of 733 Ames
   values and 1 of 138 `synth_multi` values lay outside the row band (the per-row
   standard error from 10 seeds is itself noisy, and the bimodal rows are not
   normal). With 60 seeds every one of the 258 penguins values is inside the band,
   maximum 3.64 SE; the 0.98 row fraction stands as the 10-seed criterion.

A line-by-line reading of ranger's `TreeProbability::findBestSplit*`,
`TreeRegression::findBestSplit*`, `Tree::bootstrap` and `Tree::splitNode` against
`native::forest` found no rule difference; the agreement above is the evidence that
none was missed at the scale a 10-seed test can see.

Independent baselines in `crates/tidyrustmodels-models/tests/forest.rs`: ranger's
default resolution for both tree types, determinism per seed and difference across
seeds, step-function recovery, probability rows summing to 1, parameter validation
and tuning, exact state round trip, constant-outcome forests collapsing to one leaf.

Not covered: variable importance, OOB error, `max.depth`, `sample.fraction`,
sampling without replacement, case and class weights, `splitrule` variants,

Findings, each of which produced a measurable disagreement before it was fixed:

1. **Zeros are missing values on Ames.** The first run matched the four smaller
   tasks exactly and failed Ames from the second tree on: xgboost split a 0/1 dummy
   at the sentinel cut above 1 and sent the zero rows to the *right* child, which is
   only possible if those rows were missing. They were: `workflows::fit()` estimates
   the recipe's sparsity (`recipes::.recipes_estimate_sparsity`; Ames 0.836), applies
   a fitted decision rule (`workflows:::should_use_sparsity`; "yes"), tells
   `step_dummy()` to emit sparse columns, parsnip converts the design to a
   `dgCMatrix`, and xgboost treats every absent entry, i.e. every exact zero of
   every column, including `Total_Bsmt_SF = 0`, as missing. Verified directly in R:
   the workflow fit equals `xgb.train` on the sparse matrix (difference 0) and not
   on the dense one (1.6e5). The port now carries the missing-value machinery
   (sketch and histograms over present entries only, forward and backward split
   scans, learned default directions, default child at prediction) and reproduces
   the decision (`Recipe::estimate_sparsity`, `core::sparsity::should_use_sparsity`,
   `ModelSpec::resolve_sparsity`); the decision is compared exactly on every task
   and the estimate within 2e-2 (R samples 1000 rows; 1.3e-4 observed on Ames).
2. **tune does not toggle.** With the sparse path in place, R's own `fit_resamples()`
   metrics disagreed with R's manual per-fold `fit()` models by up to 2.8 % rmse. Only
   `fit.workflow` calls `toggle_sparsity`; tune 2.1.0 fits folds through
   `.fit_pre()`/`.fit_model()` and gets dense xgboost. `Workflow::fit_resamples`
   mirrors that, and the fold boosters are compared against R's `fit()` fold models
   with their own metrics, the resampling summaries against R's `fit_resamples()`.
3. **Base score scale.** xgboost stores the binary base score as the event rate and
   applies the logit when it initialises predictions; storing the logit made every
   binary base-score row fail by 0.9 while predictions were already exact.
4. **Dense root statistics.** On dense data xgboost sums the first feature's
   histogram bins for the root gradient; on sparse data it sums the gradient pairs.
   The two orders of double accumulation differ in the last bit, which decides
   whether a feature's backward scan runs (`SplitContainsMissingValues` is an exact
   comparison) and hence the recorded default direction.

### Second pass: the toggle is a recipe operation (2026-10-13)

A failing Python interop test (a binding bug: `Table.with_sparse([])` did not clear
the flags) led to a closer look at how workflows' toggle reaches xgboost. Probed in
R (`workflows:::toggle_sparsity`, `recipes:::.recipes_toggle_sparse_args`,
`parsnip:::maybe_sparse_matrix`, and a 200-row design with 93 % zeros):

| R fact | measurement |
|---|---|
| the toggle only sets `sparse = "yes"` on steps whose argument is `"auto"`; the design is a `dgCMatrix` iff the baked predictors hold sparse vectors | a numeric-only recipe with decision "yes" fits **dense**: equal to `fit_xy()` on the dense matrix (0), max abs diff 0.038 from the sparse-input fit |
| at prediction parsnip bakes the new data and converts it again | a sparse-input fit predicting the *dense* data: max abs diff 0.87 from its sparse-data predictions, 0.86 from the dense fit (zeros read as values) |

The first port (ADR-0011 B's model policy, kept in ADR-0015's first draft) got
both cases wrong: it would have read zeros as missing on a toggled numeric-only
recipe and on dense new data after a sparse fit. ADR-0015 was amended: `StepDummy`
carries recipes' `sparse` argument, `Workflow::fit` toggles the recipe
(`ModelSpec::sparse_engine` + `Recipe::toggle_sparse`) and keeps the toggled recipe,
and the booster reads zeros as missing iff the design in front of it is sparse
(`forced_zeros_missing` only for the explicit policy).

Verification was extended rather than re-derived: the R runner now records
`fit.sparsity.design_sparse` (sparse vectors in the predictors baked by the recipe
the fitted workflow keeps) and, for sparse-input tasks, `predicted_test_dense_input`
(predictions on the test rows with the sparse columns materialised); the harness
checks the decision, the input and design sparsity and the booster's rule exactly
and compares the densified-input predictions. A third dataset (`synthetic_vsparse_reg`,
200 × 8, six predictors at 96 % zeros, one factor) carries the three cases:

| task | steps | R decision | R design sparse | Rust (exact) | L3 booster | predictions | densified input | L4 metrics (max rel) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `synth_vsparse_xgb_toggle` | (none) | **yes** | **no** (nothing to toggle) | decision, input, design, rule all match | 285 nodes exact; values <= 5.6e-8 rel | **exact (0)**, zeros as values | n/a | 3.0e-15 |
| `synth_vsparse_xgb_toggle_dummy` | dummy | **yes** | **yes** (sparse indicators) | match | 321 nodes exact; values <= 6.4e-8 abs | **exact (0)**, zeros as missing | n/a | 9.6e-16 |
| `synth_vsparse_xgb_input` | (none), sparse input | yes | **yes** (input) | match | 271 nodes exact | **exact (0)** | **exact (0)** (zeros as values) | 5.9e-16 |

The three earlier sparse xgboost tasks keep their results (`synth_sparse_xgb` and
`synth_sparse_xgb_dense`: densified-input predictions exact; `synth_sparse_clf_xgb`:
probabilities within 1e-6), and `ames_reg_xgb` (decision "yes" through
`step_dummy`) is unchanged. All fourteen boost fixtures were regenerated for the
new fields. Differential suite after the second pass: 16771 comparisons, 0
failures, 67 tasks. Python: `step_dummy(sparse=)` and the densified-prediction rule
are covered in `python/tests/test_interop.py`.

Not covered: sparse storage, ranger / glmnet with sparse input (identical in R;
ranger is level 5 here), Arrow types beyond integers, floats, strings, dictionaries
and booleans, zero-copy.

## Workflow sets (Phase 8k)

Executed 2026-10-13. workflowsets 1.1.1 was read from its sources and probed in R
(`target/wfs_probe*.log`, scratch probes `probe_wfs3.R`): `workflow_set()` lays the
combinations out preprocessor-outermost / model-innermost in the given order and
names unnamed elements by class and position (`recipe_1_linear_reg`); a
`workflow_map("tune_grid")` on a workflow without `tune()` falls back to
`fit_resamples()` (`.config` `pre0_mod0_post0`; tuned candidates carry zero-padded
`pre0_mod01_post0` labels, `make_config_labs`); `collect_metrics()` orders rows by
workflow, `.config`, then metric *name* (`rsq` after `rmse` whatever the set order);
`rank_results()` negates larger-is-better means, keeps each workflow's first best
with `select_best`, ranks with `rank(ties.method = "random")` under seed 1 and
defaults to the set's first metric; `fit_best()` needs `save_workflow = TRUE` and
refits the top workflow through `fit()`. `tidyr::crossing()` de-duplicates identical
objects (two identical recipes gave one workflow). ADR-0016 ports this; the one
deliberate difference is tie-breaking (order of appearance here).

Three tasks carry a `workflow_set` field; the R runner re-derives workflowsets' ids
from `fix_list_names` and asserts them before mapping. The harness compares ids,
info and result classes exactly, `collect_metrics` row keys exactly in order with
values within the summary bounds, ranks exactly outside tie groups and as sets inside
(three modes: the task's metric, the same with `select_best`, the default metric),
and `fit_best`'s workflow, parameters, model class and test predictions.

| task | set | best (R = Rust) | tie groups | collect_metrics (max abs / rel) | fit_best predictions |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `wfs_reg_filter` | 2 named preprocessors (filter chains) x lm, tuned rpart (3 `cost_complexity`), xgboost; 6 workflows, 10 candidates | `basic_lm` | 0 | 3.3e-16 / 7.6e-15 | 1.3e-14 rel |

Findings:

1. **The task-level model of `wfs_multi_pairs` is a tree, not the multinomial fit.**
   Its first version resampled nnet without normalisation and, on this task's split,
   nnet stopped short of the optimum (fold `mn_log_loss` 1e-5 rel from the Rust
   solution; linfa did not converge at all), and even normalised the test
   probabilities differed by 1.3e-7 against the 1e-7 bound. Within the set the
   normalised multinomial workflow is compared only on resampling summaries (bounds
   of `penguins_species`, Phase 8c, now also `[tasks.wfs_multi_pairs]`) and, as the
   winner, on `fit_best` probabilities (4.3e-9 abs).
2. **Ties are real and only ties differ.** Identical tuned-tree candidates (grids
   whose `cost_complexity` values prune nothing extra) produce tie groups in two
   tasks; R's seed-1 random ranks and the order-of-appearance ranks here agree as
   sets, and no task has a tie at the top (asserted), so `fit_best` is comparable.
3. `option_add(id = )` takes a single id (one call per workflow), and `fit_best` on
   a set mapped without `control_grid(save_workflow = TRUE)` aborts even for a
   `fit_resamples` winner.

## The differential suite on Linux and macOS (2026-10-14)

The repository was pushed to GitHub on 2026-10-14 and `.github/workflows/ci.yml` ran
the suite for the first time off the machine that produced the fixtures (R on
`x86_64-w64-mingw32`). Run 38616615538 (commit `578a958`, rustc 1.99.0, tolerances as
measured on Windows): Windows 17 542 of 17 542 comparisons pass; Linux x86_64
(ubuntu-24.04) 5 fail; macOS arm64 (macos-26-arm64) 17 fail. Every failing row is on
one of the five xgboost classification tasks; the regression boosters, all other
models, the recipes, metrics, resampling ids and tuning are identical on the three
platforms (the maxima of every other row agree to the printed digit; comparison
script in the session scratchpad, raw logs in the run).

Cause (ADR-0017): the port's sigmoid / softmax / base-score logit call `f32::exp` /
`f32::ln`, which resolve to the C runtime's `expf` / `logf`; Windows UCRT, glibc and
Apple's libm differ in the last float32 ulp, and R's xgboost on Windows shares the
UCRT result. Measured maxima of the affected quantities (max over the task's rows;
Windows in the first column for reference):

| quantity (classification boosters) | bound on the fixture platform | Windows | Linux x86_64 | macOS arm64 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| fold boosters' loss changes (rel) | 2e-7 rel / 1e-6 abs | 6.1e-7 | 2.9e-5 (`synth_multi_xgb`) | 2.9e-5 |
| fold boosters' base weights (rel) | 2e-7 rel / 1e-6 abs | 1.2e-5 (`synth_sparse_clf_xgb`, 4.2e-8 abs) | 2.8e-5 | 2.8e-5 |
| fold boosters' split values and leaves (rel) | 2e-7 rel / 1e-6 abs | 4.8e-7 | 4.6e-6 | 1.0e-5 |
| assessment probabilities (abs) | 1e-6 | 0 | 1.5e-8 | 1.2e-7 |
| per-fold `mn_log_loss` / `brier_class` (rel) | 1e-6 | 4.4e-16 | 3.3e-9 | 4.2e-9 |
| CV summary mean / std_err (rel) | 1e-12 (`native_summary`) | 1.3e-15 | 1.4e-8 | 1.4e-8 |

Decision: `tolerances.toml` gains `[platform.<os>-<arch>.tasks.<task>]` sections that
apply only on that platform (3 bounds on Linux, 11 on macOS, each set to the measured
maximum rounded up to two digits and quoting the measurement); the Windows bounds are
unchanged, so the bit-exact record above keeps being asserted where it was measured.
The report header now names the platform, the fixtures' R platform and the number of
platform bounds in force, and CI keeps `target/differential_report.md` as an artifact
per OS. **Not measured:** whether the port is bit-exact against an xgboost built on
Linux or macOS; that needs fixtures produced by R on those platforms.
